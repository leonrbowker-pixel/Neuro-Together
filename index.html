UK Special Educational Needs Law

UK EHCP (Education, Health and Care Plan) Master Guide

An EHCP is a legally binding document issued by your Local Authority (LA) under the Children and Families Act 2014. It legally mandates specified educational provision that mainstream school budgets cannot provide alone.

The Statutory 20-Week Legal Timeline

Week 0–6: Request & Decision LA has 6 weeks from your parental request to agree or refuse to assess.
Week 6–16: Assessment Evidence gathering from EP, Speech & Language, OT, and medical reports.
Week 16: Draft Plan LA issues Draft EHCP or notifies refusal to issue (triggers immediate tribunal appeal rights).
Week 20: Final Legal Plan Final EHCP legally sealed, naming specific school placement and funded hours.

Understanding & Checking Sections A to K:

Section A: Views of Parent & Child

The child's story, aspirations, and what an ordinary day looks like. Highlight sensory overwhelm, school anxiety, and masking behaviors.

Section B: Special Educational Needs (SEN)

Every single challenge (sensory processing, executive dysfunction, working memory, emotional regulation) MUST be written here. If it is not in Section B, the council does not have to fund it in Section F.

Section F: Special Educational Provision (The Golden Section)

Must be quantified and specified by law. Reject vague phrasing like "regular support from a TA" or "access to sensory breaks". Insist on exact details: "1-to-1 Teaching Assistant for 25 hours per week" or "3x 15-minute scheduled occupational sensory diet breaks daily".

Section I: Placement / School Named

Names the specific school (mainstream, enhanced resourced provision, or independent specialist). Left blank in the draft for parental preference consultation.

Clinical Nutrition & Sensory Aversion

ARFID (Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder) Guide

ARFID is not picky eating or stubbornness. It is an eating disorder driven by severe sensory hypersensitivity (texture, smell, color), fear of negative consequences (choking, vomiting), or a complete absence of appetite cues.

🤢

1. Sensory Sensitivity

Certain textures (e.g. wet or slimy vegetables) feel physically unbearable in the mouth, triggering an immediate gag reflex.

😨

2. Fear of Negative Outcomes

Intense phobia of choking, nausea, or stomach pain, often following an illness or an episode of gagging.

📉

3. Lack of Appetite Cues

Due to impaired interoception, the brain does not register hunger signals, causing them to forget or refuse meals entirely.

✅ Supportive ARFID Tactics

  • Protect Safe Foods: Always offer at least one guaranteed safe brand/texture at every meal without guilt.
  • Zero Mealtime Pressure: Never bribe, force-feed, or use "two more bites" bargaining. Pressure increases cortisol and stops swallowing.
  • Food Chaining: Introduce tiny variations from safe foods (e.g., if they accept one brand of chicken nugget, try a nearly identical breaded shape).

❌ Harmful Approaches to Avoid

  • "They'll eat when they get hungry enough": Children with ARFID will starve themselves to the point of severe malnutrition.
  • Hiding vegetables inside safe foods: If the child discovers a hidden texture, they will lose trust in the safe food and drop it forever.
