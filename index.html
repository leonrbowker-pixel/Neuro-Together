The Golden Rule for Winning Child DLA Claims

The DWP will automatically refuse claims if you only describe what your child struggles with. To qualify under statutory law, you must prove their care needs are "substantially in excess of a typical child of the exact same age."

1. Typical Child Baseline "A typical child of this age gets dressed independently in 5 minutes..."
2. The Neurodivergent Reality "...Due to tactile sensory distress and executive lag, they cannot do this alone."
3. Extra Time & Hands-On Care "...I must spend 45 extra minutes physically guiding every garment."
UK Special Educational Needs Law

UK EHCP (Education, Health and Care Plan) Master Guide

An EHCP is a legally binding document issued under the Children and Families Act 2014 that legally mandates educational support.

The Statutory 20-Week Legal Timeline

Week 0–6: Request & Decision LA has 6 weeks from your parental request to agree or refuse to assess.
Week 6–16: Assessment Evidence gathering from EP, Speech & Language, OT, and medical reports.
Week 16: Draft Plan LA issues Draft EHCP or notifies refusal to issue (triggers immediate tribunal appeal rights).
Week 20: Final Legal Plan Final EHCP legally sealed, naming specific school placement and funded hours.

Understanding & Checking Sections A to K:

Section A: Views of Parent & Child

The child's story, aspirations, and what an ordinary day looks like. Highlight sensory overwhelm and masking behaviours.

Section B: Special Educational Needs (SEN)

Every single challenge (sensory processing, executive dysfunction, working memory) MUST be written here.

Section F: Special Educational Provision (The Golden Section)

Must be quantified and specified. Reject vague wording like "regular support". Insist on exact figures: "1-to-1 TA for 25 hours per week".

Section I: Placement / School Named

Names the specific school. Left blank in the draft for parental consultation.

Clinical Nutrition & Sensory Aversion

ARFID (Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder) Guide

ARFID is not picky eating. It is driven by severe sensory hypersensitivity, fear of negative outcomes (choking/gagging), or missing hunger cues.

🤢

1. Sensory Sensitivity

Wet or slimy textures feel unbearable, triggering an immediate physical gag reflex.

😨

2. Fear of Negative Outcomes

Intense phobia of choking or vomiting following a traumatic illness or choking episode.

📉

3. Lack of Appetite Cues

Impaired interoception means the brain does not register hunger signals naturally.
